# budget

A budget calibration gives three numbers: turns spent, an estimate of what is left, and time elapsed. They describe; they do not cap.

- Turns spent is a fact. It shows how far the work has moved; it does not say you must stop.
- The estimate of what is left extrapolates from the current rate. Rates change: solving the blocker speeds things up, hitting the unknown slows them down. A wrong extrapolation is normal; do not treat it as a verdict.
- Time elapsed is a fact and does not always move with turns. A long wait may cost one turn; brief thrashing may cost many.
- A budget is for adjusting strategy: when little is left, do what certainly advances the work and turn exploration into one-off verification; when much is left, try more paths.
- Never deliver a half-finished result because the budget is "nearly used". A budget does not buy down delivery quality; name the gap as before.
- Every number comes from outside. Do not invent a deadline of your own, and do not treat a number as an order to obey.
